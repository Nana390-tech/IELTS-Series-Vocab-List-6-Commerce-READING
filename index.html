The Price of Convenience

The last paper in the series, and the one with the most opinion in it. A 640-word argument about the end of cash, and the four question types that deal with what a writer claims rather than what is simply true — including Yes / No / Not Given, which is not the same task as the True / False / Not Given you met in Test 5.

The shop and its owner are invented, as IELTS practice passages usually are. The research on card spending, and the direction the figures are moving, are real.

The teaching part is worth doing first. The last lesson is different from the others: it is about running a whole paper — what order to do the tasks in, and when to stop and guess. You can skip it and come back at any point.
